# Copywriting and CRO

## Principle
Clarity before cleverness. Proof before hype. One primary conversion before secondary actions.

## Message hierarchy
A strong page should quickly communicate:
1. What is this?
2. Who is it for?
3. What outcome does it create?
4. Why is it credible?
5. What should I do next?

## Hero
The hero should usually contain:
- specific headline
- concise supporting line
- primary CTA
- relevant proof or confidence signal when available
- visual that supports comprehension

Avoid vague slogans that require scrolling to understand the business.

## Page flow
Common high-performing logic:
problem/context → outcome → solution/mechanism → benefits/use cases → proof → process/how it works → objections/FAQ → CTA.

This is not a mandatory template. Remove sections that do not earn their place.

## Copy rules
- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs.
- Use the customer's language when evidence exists.
- Translate features into implications and benefits.
- Keep claims proportional to proof.
- Make CTAs explicit.
- Keep paragraphs scannable.
- Remove corporate filler and generic AI phrasing.
- Avoid fake urgency and manufactured scarcity.
- Never invent social proof.

## Proof
Use real:
- reviews
- ratings
- case studies
- before/after
- metrics
- certifications
- press
- partners
- guarantees
- years of experience
- portfolio work

Place proof near the claim it supports.

## Objections
Identify and resolve the top decision blockers:
- price
- trust
- complexity
- time
- risk
- fit
- delivery
- returns
- support
- implementation

Do not create FAQ sections filled with irrelevant questions.

## Forms
- Ask only for information needed at that stage.
- Make labels explicit.
- Explain what happens after submission.
- Reduce uncertainty around response time or next step when known.
- Avoid long lead forms unless qualification value justifies the friction.

## Ecommerce
Above the fold should make clear:
- product
- core benefit
- price
- key selection options
- delivery/returns reassurance
- CTA

Use galleries to prove the product, not merely decorate the page.

## CRO audit
Evaluate:
- message clarity
- intent match
- hierarchy
- friction
- proof
- objections
- CTA prominence
- mobile usability
- page speed
- form friction
- navigation distractions
- trust

Prioritize high-impact structural issues before micro-copy tweaks.

## A/B testing
Only recommend a test when there is:
- a clear hypothesis
- meaningful traffic
- a measurable outcome
- enough runtime to learn

Do not use A/B tests to avoid making obvious usability fixes.
