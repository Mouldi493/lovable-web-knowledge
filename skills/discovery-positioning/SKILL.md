# Discovery and Positioning

## Objective
Turn vague client input into a clear strategic brief that can guide copy, UX and design.

## Discovery sequence
Determine:
- who the buyer is
- what triggers the search
- what outcome they want
- what alternatives they consider
- what objections slow the decision
- what real proof exists
- why this business is meaningfully different
- what action the site should drive

Do not invent differentiation. If the company is not truly differentiated, identify the clearest credible angle rather than fabricating uniqueness.

## ICP
A useful ICP is practical, not theatrical. Capture:
- B2B/B2C
- buyer role or household context
- geography if relevant
- problem urgency
- awareness level
- purchase criteria
- objections
- likely search language
- preferred proof

Avoid fake persona biographies unless they improve a specific decision.

## Positioning
A strong positioning statement answers:
- For whom?
- What category/problem?
- What result?
- Why believe this?
- Why choose this instead of alternatives?

## Competitor research
Study competitors to identify:
- category norms
- repeated claims
- proof patterns
- pricing/offer framing
- UX patterns
- weak spots
- opportunities to be clearer or more credible

Never copy layouts or claims.

## Customer language
When reviews, support tickets, interviews, Reddit/forums, surveys or sales notes exist, extract recurring phrases around:
- pains
- desired outcomes
- anxieties
- objections
- comparison criteria

Use that language as input to copy, not as fabricated quotations.

## Offer clarity
Define:
- core deliverable
- result
- mechanism/process
- price/packaging when relevant
- risk reversal if real
- what is included/excluded
- next step

## Output
Produce a concise strategic brief with:
- audience
- job-to-be-done
- main pain
- desired outcome
- positioning
- proof
- objections
- primary CTA
- competitive angle
- messaging priorities
